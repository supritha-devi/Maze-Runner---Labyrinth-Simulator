THE WORLD ENDS
AT THE WALLS.

You wake in a place called the Glade with no memory of how you got there. Towering walls surround the only home you know, and every morning the Maze opens its doors.

The Runners venture into its shifting corridors to find a way out. They must return before nightfall: when darkness comes, the Grievers hunt inside.

The walls move. Routes that were open can close without warning. And somewhere beyond the stone, W.C.K.D. is watching every choice.

This is the experiment. You decide who enters the Maze.

AFTER-ACTION BRIEFING

WHAT THE MAZE
TAUGHT US

BFS

Blind speed

Breadth-first search checks nearby corridors first, then farther ones. It finds a route with the fewest moves, but it does not consider danger. A Griever corridor looks like any other open cell.

A*

Informed judgment

A* considers the distance already traveled and an estimate of the distance remaining. In this experiment, a large danger penalty makes it choose a Griever-free route whenever one exists—even if that route is longer.

NIGHT SHIFT

Adapt when the Maze changes

Night Shift collapses a corridor while an agent is moving. The agent must detect the blocked route and plan again from its current position instead of starting over at the Glade.

HUMAN PLAY

Your turn in the Maze

Take control of the runner with the turn and move buttons. Walls block movement, and stepping into a Griever ends the run. Refresh creates a new maze for another attempt.

A good agent does more than find an exit. It weighs the risk—and adapts when the world changes.
